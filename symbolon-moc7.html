1. Models of Consciousness: Fireside Chat

When: Sunday, October 11, 10:30 AM

With: Lenore Blum, Johannes Kleiner, Camilo Miguel Signorelli

Abstract

On the eve of the seventh Models of Consciousness conference (MoC7), this opening session brings together three people involved in the conference and the Association for Mathematical Consciousness Science (AMCS), which organises it. Since its first edition in Oxford in 2019, the conference has travelled from an online edition to Stanford, Oxford, Bamberg, Hokkaido, and now Copenhagen. The conversation will cover how the series and the association came about, what a mathematical approach to consciousness involves, and how the field has changed over these editions. It will also introduce this year’s programme: its four themes (philosophical foundations; methodologies and measures; AI, LLMs and consciousness science; phenomenology), and a new collaborative project, in which participants will work towards a shared paper on concepts, methodological standards and future priorities for the field.

Lenore Blum is Distinguished Career Professor of Computer Science, Emerita, at Carnegie Mellon University. Her research has moved from logic and model theory to a theory of computation over the real numbers. More recently, with Manuel and Avrim Blum, she has worked on the Conscious Turing Machine, a formal model of consciousness from the perspective of theoretical computer science. She was elected inaugural President of the AMCS in 2021 and sits on the MoC7 advisory board. She has also worked throughout her career to increase the participation of women in mathematics and computer science.

Johannes Kleiner is a mathematician and physicist working on mathematical approaches to consciousness. After a PhD in mathematics at the University of Regensburg, he completed a second doctorate at LMU Munich on what is now called mathematical consciousness science. He is based at the Institute for Psychology of the University of Bamberg. He was the founding chair of the AMCS board and is part of the MoC7 organising committee.

Camilo Miguel Signorelli studies the structure of conscious experience from phenomenological, mathematical and life-centred perspectives, and its implications for physical theories and AI. His work spans mathematical phenomenology, compositional models of consciousness and the neuroscience of consciousness, with past collaborations at the University of Oxford and INSERM-CEA (UNICOG). He is now at the Center for Philosophy of Artificial Intelligence, University of Copenhagen, and is one of the MoC7 organisers.

3. Empathy, Morality and Care

When: Monday, October 12, 4:00 PM

Presented by: Dan Zahavi

Abstract

Empathy is often presented as a moral force, a counter to selfishness and indifference that should be cultivated and extended. Recent critics have challenged this view. Paul Bloom argues that empathy is biased and distorts moral judgement, and Nils Bubandt and Rane Willerslev point to its use in deception and manipulation. This talk argues that these critiques rely on two common definitions of empathy: as affective sharing (feeling what the other feels), or as imaginative perspective-taking (putting oneself in the other’s shoes). Drawing on the early phenomenological tradition (Husserl, Scheler, Stein), it proposes a different account. On this account, empathy is a direct experiential acquaintance with another person’s experience in the face-to-face encounter, one that preserves rather than removes the difference between self and other. Empathy understood this way is not sufficient for morality, and as Scheler noted, cruelty presupposes it. It is nonetheless not irrelevant, since it gives us a basic grasp of others as co-subjects. The talk ends with the clinical setting, where empathic openness can protect against projecting one’s own views onto the patient. It also asks what it would mean to train empathy.

Dan Zahavi is Professor of Philosophy at the University of Copenhagen and Director of the Center for Subjectivity Research, which he has led since its founding in 2002. From 2018 to 2021 he was also Professor of Philosophy at the University of Oxford. His work centres on phenomenology and philosophy of mind, in particular selfhood, intersubjectivity, empathy and collective intentionality, often in dialogue with psychology and psychiatry. His books include Subjectivity and Selfhood (2005), The Phenomenological Mind (with Shaun Gallagher, 2008), Self and Other (2014), Husserl’s Legacy (2017), Phenomenology: The Basics (2019) and Being We (2025). He was co-editor-in-chief of Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences from 2007 to 2025, and he received an ERC Advanced Grant in 2019. He is also a member of the MoC7 advisory board.

4. Accounting for Qualia in the Natural World

When: Tuesday, October 13, 4:00 PM

Presented by: Paul G. Skokowski

Abstract

Explaining qualia in physical terms is what is often called the “hard problem” of consciousness. This talk addresses the qualia of sensation through sensory naturalism, the thesis that our senses are detectors of physical properties in the world. This view challenges the common assumption that qualia can be understood by studying only the brain or computational mechanisms. The talk shows how the theory keeps multiple realization, and considers what it implies for whether artificial systems could have sensory experiences. It draws on his recent book Sensing Qualia: Solving the Hard Problem of Consciousness (University of Chicago Press, 2026).

Paul G. Skokowski is a philosopher and cognitive scientist. He teaches in the Symbolic Systems Program at Stanford University, where he is also Executive Director and co-founder of the Center for the Explanation of Consciousness, and he is a Fellow in Philosophy at St Edmund Hall, University of Oxford. His work focuses on how sensory experience can be explained within the physical sciences. His most recent book is Sensing Qualia: Solving the Hard Problem of Consciousness (University of Chicago Press, 2026).
